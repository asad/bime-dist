The Molecular Editor
for the Modern Web

BioInception Molecular Editor — open source, zero dependencies.

  • Draw molecules and reactions, with mapping labels
  • 1181 built-in drugs & natural products, one click to load
  • Full SMILES, SMARTS, MOL V2000/V3000 support
  • CIP R/S and E/Z stereo assignment built in
  • Pure JavaScript — embed in any page, works offline

Everything you need to draw chemistry

A complete cheminformatics toolkit delivered as a single embeddable JavaScript library.

1181 Molecules

Curated compound library built in — one click to load any compound into the editor.

SMILES & SMARTS

Full parser/writer with strict validation, canonical output, and substructure search.

Reactions

Draw reactions with reaction arrows and atom-mapping labels for didactic clarity.

Zero Dependencies

Pure JS, runs fully offline. Embed on any page without build tools or npm.

Export Anywhere

SVG, PNG, MOL V2000/V3000, clipboard copy — one call each.

CIP Stereo

R/S and E/Z assignment built in, following IUPAC CIP priority rules.

Full capability list

✓Strict SMILES parser + validator
✓Canonical SMILES (connectivity-based canonicalization)
✓Aromatic perception (Huckel 4n+2)
✓MOL V2000 & V3000 export
✓Reaction arrows + manual atom-mapping labels
✓SMSD MCS & similarity (in-browser)
✓SSSR ring perception + fused rings
✓69 public scaffold templates
✓Stereochemistry (wedge/dash, @, E/Z)
✓10 interactive drawing tools
✓SMARTS substructure search
✓CIP R/S and E/Z stereochemistry
✓SVG & PNG image export
✓Local molecule naming (offline)
✓1181 built-in molecule database
✓Undo/redo + keyboard shortcuts
✓Dark mode + fully responsive
✓Touch & pinch-to-zoom support

Embed in 10 lines

Drop these scripts on any page and the editor is ready. No build step, no npm, no config.

<script src="https://asad.github.io/bime-dist/editor/Molecule.js"></script>
<script src="https://asad.github.io/bime-dist/editor/Layout.js"></script>
<script src="https://asad.github.io/bime-dist/editor/Templates.js"></script>
<script src="https://asad.github.io/bime-dist/editor/SmilesParser.js"></script>
<script src="https://asad.github.io/bime-dist/editor/SmilesWriter.js"></script>
<script src="https://asad.github.io/bime-dist/editor/MolfileWriter.js"></script>
<script src="https://asad.github.io/bime-dist/editor/Renderer.js"></script>
<script src="https://asad.github.io/bime-dist/editor/History.js"></script>
<script src="https://asad.github.io/bime-dist/editor/Tools.js"></script>
<script src="https://asad.github.io/bime-dist/editor/MolEditor.js"></script>

<div id="editor" style="width:100%;height:460px"></div>

<script>
var editor = new MolEditor('editor', '100%', '460px');
editor.readGenericMolecularInput('c1ccccc1');
</script>

A Personal Dedication

BIME is released in the year I turn 50 — a small gift back to the research and student community that has given me so much.

I am deeply grateful to my mentors — Prof. Seyed E. Hasnain, Prof. Dietmar Schomburg, Prof. Christoph Steinbeck, and Prof. Dame Janet Thornton — whose guidance, insight, and uncompromising standards laid the foundation of this work and shaped the scientist I became.

I am equally indebted to the colleagues I was privileged to work alongside during my doctoral and postdoctoral years, whose curiosity, generous exchange of ideas, and unwavering dedication were a daily source of inspiration and shaped the way I think about science to this day.

To the next generation of students and early-career researchers: I hope BIME sparks in you the same curiosity that was once kindled in me.

— Syed Asad Rahman
